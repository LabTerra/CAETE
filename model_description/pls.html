Plant Life Strategy (PLS)

In CAETÊ, Plant Life Strategies (PLS) are the basic units of vegetation representation. Ecologically, each PLS corresponds to an average individual or a population of individuals sharing identical functional traits. Since CAETÊ is an area-based model rather than an individual-based model, each PLS is characterized by simulated trait values for a given area, not for a single individual.

The PLS is constructed by uniformly sampling trait values from empirically derived ranges. Conceptually, the full range of each trait forms an axis of an n-dimensional hypervolume (Fig. 1; Blonder, 2017; Villéger et al. 2008; Rius et al. 2023). Every point inside this hypervolume corresponds to a unique combination of trait values and thus to a distinct PLS. The sampled points delineate a potential functional space containing tens of thousands of candidate PLSs.

As in other trait-based models (e.g., Pavlick et al., 2013; Reu et al., 2011; Scheiter, Langan, Higgins, 2013), sampling an appropriate number of PLSs from the potential functional space, combined with an environmental filtering mechanism, enables CAETÊ to reproduce consistent patterns of functional and biogeochemical diversity.

Creating a PLS

The generation of PLS involves interdependence among certain functional traits. For example, the carbon allocation fractions to different plant tissues must form a numerical combination whose total sums to 1 (or 100%), representing the entirety of available carbon for allocation. Carbon residence times can be defined as pseudo-random values sampled from known distributions. These six traits (allocation fractions and carbon residence times in leaf, fine root, and woody tissue pools) determine the main allometric relationships characterizing each PLS. Although other variables also contribute to defining structural characteristics, such as height, stem diameter, and crown size, these traits form the functional basis of each generated PLS. The sequential steps for creating a PLS are described below. Note that some steps (1 and 3) are performed only once, with generated data reused (sampled) for subsequent life strategy creations:

1. Generation of carbon allocation combinations (%):

For woody strategies, allocation rates to leaves (aleaf), fine roots (aroot), and woody tissues (awood) are combined so that their sum is exactly 100% (i.e., aleaf + aroot + awood = 100). This rule is applied exhaustively among elements of three ordered vectors (aleaf, aroot, and awood) with values ranging from 10 to 90 in increments of 0.0125. For non-woody strategies, where there is no allocation to woody tissues (awood = 0), combinations are generated only between aleaf and aroot, respecting the same total-sum criterion. In these cases, aleaf and aroot values range from 20% to 80%, also in 0.0125 increments. All valid combinations are stored for use in subsequent steps.

2. Combining allocation rates and carbon residence times:

This step defines the first allometric relationships of the PLS to be generated. First, a randomly selected valid allocation-rate vector (generated in step I) is chosen. Then, carbon residence times in the three main pools (leaves, fine roots, and woody tissues) are assigned to this vector. Residence times are sampled as pseudo-random numbers from uniform distributions bounded by specific minima and maxima. For leaves and fine roots, residence times range from 0.08333 to 4 years. For woody tissues, the interval ranges from 0.08333 to 80 years. All values are expressed in years and follow a continuous uniform distribution within these limits. Both the defined intervals for allocation rates and carbon residence times are based on values reported by Pavlick et al. (2013).

3. Creation of N:C and P:C ratios in plant tissues:

Defining nitrogen-to-carbon (N:C) and phosphorus-to-carbon (P:C) ratios in plant tissues also involves trait interdependence, since the N:P ratio has a biologically constrained range among organisms. The generation of these combinations follows a similar logic to that applied in step I. For each pool (leaves, fine roots, and woody tissues), N:C and P:C ratios are combined so that the resulting N:P ratio falls within empirically compatible values. Specifically, for leaves and fine roots, N:C values range from \(5\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{g\,N\,g^{-1}\,C}\) to \(0.06\,\mathrm{g\,N\,g^{-1}\,C}\), while P:C values range from \(2\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm{g\,P\,g^{-1}\,C}\) to \(8\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{g\,P\,g^{-1}\,C}\). For woody tissues, these intervals are reduced by an order of magnitude, assuming lower nutrient concentrations in these compartments. Valid combinations are obtained through an exhaustive scan of ordered discrete N:C and P:C vectors, followed by filtering combinations that satisfy the N:P constraint. Resulting combinations are stored and later randomly sampled for incorporation into the carbon allocation and residence-time combinations. With these nutrient ratios added, the PLS at this point contains 12 elements, representing allocation fractions, residence times, and relative N and P concentrations in each of the three pools. Limits used in this process are based on leaf nutrient observational data by Reich and Oleksyn (2004). For practical purposes, fine roots are assumed to have elemental composition similar to leaves, while woody tissues have N and P concentrations approximately ten times lower.

4. Checking PLS viability as a carbon accumulator:

To verify the viability of the newly created PLS (PLS vector) as a valid functional strategy, we simulate a system with a constant carbon input fixed at \(1.0\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{kg\,C\,m^{-2}\,day^{-1}}\). This input is distributed among the three pools (leaves, fine roots, and woody tissues (for woody plants) or leaves and fine roots for grasses) according to proportions defined in step I. PLS vector is then used to dynamically calculate carbon accumulation in each pool over time. After a 3,000-day simulation period, the vector is considered viable if carbon stocks in leaves and fine roots are simultaneously equal to or greater than the daily carbon input minimum. If this condition is met, the vector is approved and assigned remaining functional traits (independently and uncorrelated), completing its functional identity and becoming a full Plant Life Strategy.
